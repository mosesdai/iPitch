## 11. Competition posture long form (for deep-talk)

China smartphone battlefield first principle: **premium mindshare is no longer monopolized by one brand**. After Huawei’s supply-chain and product narrative swing-back, urban consumers again have a “domestic flagship is respectable enough” option; vivo, OPPO, Xiaomi cut young and family budgets via camera, mall retail, and ecosystem price anchors. Apple still owns strong retail aesthetics, privacy, and cross-device loop — but if those advantages only flash in launch seasons and otherwise drown under daily short-video content, “brand everyday” loses to “algorithm everyday”.

Samsung China is the counterexample to keep in the drawer: globally it mirrors Apple; sports and Olympics-grade assets are not scarce; yet China phone share has long been hard to convert — reminding us **global brand campaigns do not automatically become a China OS**. Amazon is another peer type: main-package spend defines “how tech owns sports”, but that definition happens on the U.S. media-rights table; copying it into “Apple should also buy NBA U.S. rights” is strategic misalignment. ByteDance is more like air: it may not steal your counter, but it steals your narrative half-life.

So competitor-chapter ops advice is not “pick one enemy and trash them”, but see weapons per war layer: share war → product & channel; sports-sub definition → rights & productization; attention war → ownable scenes. NBA China’s legal home field is layer three crossed with retail; it may occasionally influence layer-two “product narrative grammar”, but never replaces the client fighting layer-one supply chain.
